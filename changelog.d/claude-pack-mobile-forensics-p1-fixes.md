### Fixed: the mobile pack's filters no longer answer questions about text nobody can see

An independent review of `sqlite_freespace`, `manifest_db` and `protobuf_peek` found two ways to learn a withheld string one bit at a time, and a third that printed some of its characters.

- `sqlite_freespace`: `contains` runs on fragment text that is only ever written to the sealed values file, but a fragment on which the expression ran for more than two seconds was counted in `scanned.contains_timeouts` and `problem_kinds`, and cost two seconds of wall time. An expression that backtracks only when a lookahead holds therefore answered "does this fragment contain my guess" once per call. `contains` now accepts only an expression of bounded cost (no `*`, `+` or `{m,}`, no repeat of a choice, no lookahead, lookbehind or back reference, at most 256 characters, a worst case of at most 16384 steps), nothing reports a time-out any more (the count and the problem kind are gone), a fragment the expression could not be decided on in time is written to the values file as if it had matched, and the page where `max_seconds` stops a run no longer depends on the time an expression took. Measured at 32,768 characters of repeated letters, the slowest of 797 accepted random expressions took 0.9 s, and a guarded expression took the same time with the guess present and absent.
- `manifest_db`: `contains` and `domain` were applied to the real relative path and domain, while the answer printed a recovery-password-shaped component as a placeholder, so `entry_count` told whether a guessed prefix of the hidden component was right (10 calls a digit). Both filters now match the text the answer prints. A `file` cell (the keyed archive) is read only when it is a BLOB of at most 1 MiB: a larger cell is not read (`metadata_status: too_large`, with its size), a cell that is not a BLOB is said without being read, and rows with no readable metadata are counted in an observation. Three rows holding a 29 MB archive took 399 MB of resident memory (the review measured 1.05 GB for a 76 MB one) and now take 22 MB. The time-limit stop is now an observation (`max_seconds (N) passed after R rows`), which also removes a flaky test: the old test passed only when the deadline fell inside a row's expression.
- `protobuf_peek`: the acceptance rule held for the whole payload of a withheld field, but `offset` and `length` could slide a window across it, and the rows printed for an accepted sub-window are the token's characters read as keys, lengths and varints. On tokens inside a message, 13 to 99 percent of 12- to 64-character tokens had a window that was shown, and 3 to 20 percent of a token's characters were derivable from what was printed (the `about one in a hundred thousand` comment was about 50 times too optimistic). A window must now not be entirely printable text, and a window aimed with `offset` or `length` must be at least 16 bytes with at least four fields. Measured again with 1,000 tokens per kind: 0 to 1.2 percent of tokens have a window that is shown, at most 0.2 percent of a token's characters are derivable, never a whole token. A small message read at an `offset` now needs four fields to be printed; the values file of a `secret_output` job still holds its rows.
- `sqlite_freespace` and `protobuf_peek` declare `secret_output: {"when_args": ["write_values"]}` (they had no declaration) and refuse `write_values` in a job that was not run as secret output (`SECRET_OUTPUT` not 1); `manifest_db` writes no values file. They answer SIGTERM, SIGINT and SIGHUP with one JSON answer (`status: failed`, `interrupted`), exit 128 plus the signal, the pages already written closed and named, and the working copy of `Manifest.db` removed. All three say `status_basis`, and `protobuf_peek` says `status` (it had only `structure.status`).
- The skills, the manifests and the README say what the filters now match, what the windows now need and that the device identifiers in the plain plists (serial number, UDID, IMEI, ICCID, phone number) are printed; the backup skill used to say that none of its reads prints a secret.

### Fixed: the tool test helpers no longer crash on a child that never reads its input

`runPy`, `runSh` and `runPySnippet` wrote their JSON to a child's stdin with no error listener, so a fixture builder that exits without reading it (every `runPySnippet` call that passes `null`) could end the whole test file with `write EPIPE` on a loaded runner. The helpers ignore the error of a child that exited, and `null` now means no input at all.
