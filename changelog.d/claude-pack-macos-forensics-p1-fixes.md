### Fixed: knowledgec_query stops at its own clock, and fsevents_parse reads a file of many gzip members in linear time (macos-forensics)

An independent review of the pack's four tools found two reads that could outrun their time limit and be killed with nothing to show.

- **`knowledgec_query` has a deadline.** It had none: a database of 2.3 million rows (697 MB, inside the 2 GiB staging default) was still being read when the 125-second cap of the review killed it, with no JSON on standard output and a 2.6 GB partial `out_file`. `max_seconds` (default 80, at most 90, below the manifest's 120) is now checked by a progress handler inside the statement, so even the sort of a table with millions of rows is stopped, and every 256 rows. When it passes the answer is `partial`, the entries are a prefix of the result in start order (whole in the file the answer names, and `out_file` is kept), and `resume` gives `rows_read`, `stopped_after_z_pk`, `last_start_raw` and `resume_since`, a microsecond before the last start read: run again with that `since` and the rows at the last start are read twice, none is skipped (a test runs the two halves and counts every row). Measured on the review's database with the cap at 125 seconds: before, killed at 125 s, 122 s of CPU, 0 bytes of output; after, rc 0 at 82 s (81 s of CPU, 130 MB resident), one JSON answer, `partial`, 230,912 entries and a 308 MB file of whole rows, `resume` given (how many rows 80 seconds read depends on the load of the machine, which was high). The cost per row is higher now, since every string passes the boundary below.
- **`fsevents_parse` finds the gzip member of a page by bisection.** `member_at()` rebuilt the list of every member's offset for every page: 100,000 members (a 2.8 MB file) took 46 seconds and about 270,000 passed the manifest's 300; the offsets are kept in a list of their own now. After: 100,000 members take 1 second and 270,000 (7.5 MB) 3. A test reads the CPU of 20,000 and of 80,000 members and fails when four times the members costs more than nine times the CPU.
- **`fsevents_parse` has a deadline and a restricted `contains`.** `max_seconds` (default 200, at most 250, below the manifest's 300): when it passes the file in hand is closed as partial at the page it was reading (`problems` names the offset), the files after it are `not_attempted` in `per_file`, `time_limit` says how to go on, and what was decoded is in the file. `contains` ran an unbounded regular expression over a path chosen by whoever wrote the volume: `(a|aa)+$` over a path of 40 characters took 37 seconds (14 s at 38), and the growth is x2.6 per two characters. It now runs a restricted subset (at most 256 characters; no back-reference, conditional, lookaround, atomic group or possessive repeat; no repeat of 9 or more inside any repeat, no repeat at all and no alternation inside a repeat of 9 or more; at most two repeats of 9 or more, at most eight repeats of any size), refused as JSON with the reason otherwise (thirteen catastrophic patterns are tested, each refused in milliseconds), and each search is given 0.5 second: a path that runs past it is counted in `filters.contains_timeouts` and the answer is `partial`. The subset cannot rule out the polynomial cases (`[a/]*[a/]*b` over 200,000 characters), which the timer bounds.

### Security: fsevents_parse, knowledgec_query and unified_log judge what they print by the shared boundary, and the tools that write values refuse a job that is not secret output (macos-forensics)

These three tools printed every path, stream name, value, column, URL and log message from the evidence whole (1,000 of 1,000 planted messages in the preview of `unified_log`, every path of `fsevents_parse`, every non-null ZOBJECT cell of `knowledgec_query`), and their manifests carried no SENSITIVE sentence.

- **One boundary on every way out.** The windows-forensics pack's shared boundary block (an allow-list per field type, a deny-list for free text, a second check written apart from both; the owner-approved hybrid), copied byte for byte into the three tools; `tests/pack-macos-forensics-boundary.test.ts` holds the copies equal to the windows one, and the stop block equal across the four tools. Every key and value of an answer, of an error answer, and of every row of a file the tool writes (`out_file`, the paging files) is judged by the type of the field it fills: paths, stream names and subsystems are names or paths (a component by component grammar), values, messages and URLs are free text, a BLOB's head is hexadecimal (`_hex`, no longer base64: the block decodes hexadecimal and judges its printable text) and the digests and times are typed. A withheld text is `<withheld N characters: reason>`; a string the second check had to replace makes a complete answer `partial` and is listed under `boundary.replaced_at`. The reader's own output file of `unified_log` is the reader's: only the preview and the answer are judged, and the manifest says so.
- **`contains` of `fsevents_parse` is matched against the path as printed**, never against what is withheld, so a piece of a withheld name matches nothing and the count cannot test a guess; a record that is not matched and has text withheld in its path is counted in `filters.contains_blind_records` and makes the answer `partial` (the count is the same whatever the needle is). The answer no longer echoes the pattern.
- **Measured** (rates, not guarantees): 24 shapes planted 1,000 times each in the slots each tool prints (a path; a value, another column, a BLOB's head and a column name; a message, a process path and a subsystem), one seeded run per tool; the test plants 300 per shape and asserts a bound per family. Of the 21 shapes the boundary claims, 20 were printed whole in 0 of 1,000 plants in `fsevents_parse` and `knowledgec_query` and 18 in `unified_log`; random base62 of 24 characters 1 of 1,000 in all three; in `unified_log` base64 keys of 16 bytes 11 of 1,000 (1.1%: a `/` splits the key into pieces under 16 characters in a path or a name) and of 20 bytes 2 of 1,000. A piece of 12 characters of an AWS-shaped key was printed in 1 or 2 plants of 1,000. Not claimed, and printed in 1,000 of 1,000: a plain password, a four-word passphrase and a UUID-shaped key. Costs: on 60,000 real paths of a development Mac, 1.2% of the 30,658 outside `Library/Caches` had a component withheld and 31.8% of the 29,342 under it (hash-named cache entries; 7.1% of all paths were replaced whole by the second check, so a log full of cache entries ends `partial`); sudo's `PWD=` in a log line is taken for a password's name and its value is withheld; under hostile input of 4,096 and 32,768 characters (14 shapes each) each tool judged them in bounded CPU time.
- **`write_values` and `export_oversize` are declared and checked.** `plist_read`, `fsevents_parse` (new `write_values`: the originals of what the boundary withheld, to `fsevents_parse-values.jsonl`, mode 0600, created first) and `knowledgec_query` (`write_values`, new, and `export_oversize`) declare `secret_output: {"when_args": [...]}` in their manifests and refuse as JSON, before anything is made, a job the harness does not run as secret output (`SECRET_OUTPUT` is not `1`) and a run outside a job. `export_oversize` used to write whole cell values to `work/<id>/tool-output` outside a job, and `plist_read` wrote its values file in a plain job; both are refused there now. `tests/pack-macos-forensics-boundary.test.ts` runs every refusal and the accepted request in a secret job.
- The skills of the pack (`artifacts/knowledgec`, `artifacts/fsevents`, `logs/unified`, `artifacts/plists`) carry the sentence: withholding is by shape, a secret of another shape is printed, and a file whose secrets matter is read in a job run with `secret_output: true`.

### Fixed: the four macos-forensics tools answer once on SIGTERM, SIGINT and SIGHUP, and clean up (macos-forensics)

A signal 1.5 seconds into a slow run left standard output empty and the exit code at minus the signal in all four tools, a staged copy of the evidence database (up to 2 GiB) in `$OUT/knowledgec-stage`, a staged copy of `/private/var/db` (up to 16 GiB) in `unified_log`'s output directory, and `unified_log`'s reader running after the tool was gone. A shared stop block (held equal in the four tools by a test) now ends each tool the way its time limit does: one JSON answer with `status: partial` and `interrupted` set to the signal's name, exit 128 plus the signal, what was staged removed, the reader the tool started ended (its own child, never a search by name), and a row of a result file written whole or not at all (the signals wait for the write). In `knowledgec_query` the statement is aborted by its progress handler, even while it sorts; `unified_log` counts what the reader wrote for at most 5 seconds. `tests/pack-macos-forensics-signals.test.ts` sends each of the three signals to each tool in the middle of its work (12 cases): the answer, the exit code, the rows, the staged copy and the reader's pid.

### Fixed: smaller corrections to the macos-forensics tools

- **`plist_read` no longer quotes the file in a failed row.** plistlib's message quotes the text that failed: `<key>password</key><integer>CANARY</integer>`, a `<real>` and a key with no value came out in the row's `error` in 100 of 100 plants of each. The row carries the class of the error (for an XML syntax error the reader's fixed text, the line and the column); in a secret job `write_values` puts the whole message in the values file under the row's `finding_id`. Its key-name list is widened by pre-shared, shared, auth and access keys, cookies, passcodes, bearer and one-time-password names, and by hint names (`hint`, `hint_text`, `UserHint`): on 67,260 property lists of a development Mac (933,552 distinct key names) it withholds 1,493 names, 211 more than before (some are not secrets: cipher-suite names, HTTP cookie constants). The walk of a directory is timed too, and `max_seconds` is clamped to 100.
- **`knowledgec_query` reads a TEXT cell through a blob handle.** `substr()` makes SQLite load the whole cell: a 300 MB TEXT value cost 325 MB of memory, now 31 MB (a BLOB was already read by its head). The handles go through a second connection that is replaced every 20,000, because CPython keeps a weak reference to each handle until its connection closes (369 MB for 300,000 rows with one connection, 122 MB with the replacement). The module's docstring and the manifest, which said no cell was ever loaded whole, are corrected (on a Python before 3.11 a statement still reads a TEXT cell whole). A database whose schema holds text that is not UTF-8 is a JSON refusal, not a `UnicodeDecodeError` (SQLite's own error message could not be decoded). A garbage `-journal` is no longer reported as a rolled-back transaction. `out_file` is created with mode 0600 in `plist_read`, `fsevents_parse` and `knowledgec_query`.
- **`unified_log` previews an entry under the Linux reader's field names too** (`time`, `message`, `raw_message`, `pid`, `thread_id`, `event_type`, `log_type`): the preview of a Mandiant read was a process, a subsystem and a category. A record that is an entry by a key whose value is empty is previewed by its key names, not as the whole row. `timeout_seconds` is one budget for staging, the census and the reader (they were not subtracted: 1,100 seconds for the reader, then the staging), the counting of the output follows with 70 seconds of its own, its sha256 is given up 85 seconds after the budget (null and a problem), and `budget` in the answer says how it was spent. A reader that exits 0 and writes to its error stream gets a warning.
- **Every answer carries `status` and `status_basis`**, `status` being `complete`, `partial` or `failed` in all four tools: `fsevents_parse` said `unsupported` and `unified_log` said `empty` (now `failed` and `partial`, with `files_by_state.unsupported` and `reader_outcome: empty` keeping the finer word), and a refusal is `failed` with a basis. The parser versions are `plist_read/4`, `fsevents_parse/4`, `knowledgec_query/4` and `unified_log/4`.
- The goal template `mac-compromise` asks how "the suspected actor" first reached the machine, where the artefacts show it (it said "the operator", the harness's word for the authorised human).
- The pack is 1.5.0; `tests/pack-macos-forensics-{deadline,signals,boundary,fixes}.test.ts` fail on the previous tools.
