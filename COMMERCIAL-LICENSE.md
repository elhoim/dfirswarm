# Commercial licence

DFIR Swarm is published under the [GNU Affero General Public License v3 or
later](LICENSE). For most people that is the end of it and costs nothing. A
commercial licence exists for the cases where it is not.

## You almost certainly do not need one

The AGPL asks nothing of you for running this software, however you run it and
whatever you charge your own clients for the work you do with it. In
particular, none of these needs a commercial licence:

- **Running it on your own machines**, in a lab, on a laptop, in a data centre
  or in an air-gapped room.
- **Working client cases with it**, as a consultancy, an incident response
  firm, an MSSP or an internal team, and billing for that work. What you sell
  is the investigation. The AGPL has nothing to say about it.
- **Modifying it for yourself**, as long as the modified version stays inside
  your organisation and nobody outside interacts with it over a network.
- **Publishing your changes**, forking it, teaching from it, or writing about
  it.

If you are wondering whether your use is fine, it very probably is.

## When you do need one

The AGPL has two conditions that a closed product cannot meet. Section 13
says that if users interact with your modified version over a network, you
must offer them that version's source. Section 5 says that a work you
distribute containing this program is covered by the same licence. So:

- **Offering a modified version as a service** to people outside your
  organisation, without publishing your modifications.
- **Shipping a product that contains this program**, or a derivative of it,
  under terms of your own.
- **Embedding it in a closed platform** whose source you cannot release.
- **An organisation whose policy forbids AGPL software**, which is a real
  constraint in some enterprises regardless of what the licence actually
  requires of them.

A commercial licence grants the same software under terms that permit those
things, and carries a support and indemnity arrangement the AGPL explicitly
disclaims.
