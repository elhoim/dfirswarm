# Trademark policy

The code is AGPL-3.0-or-later. The name is not.

Section 7 of the [licence](LICENSE) says this may be done: 7(e) allows
declining to grant rights in trade names, trademarks and service marks, and
7(c) allows requiring that modified versions be marked as different from the
original. This policy is those two terms, written out. It is deliberate: the
licence is what lets you take this code and do what you like with it, and the name is what lets a reader tell this project from a
copy of it that behaves differently. Both matter to a forensic tool, where
somebody may one day have to say in writing which software produced a result.

**The marks.** "DFIR Swarm" and the project wordmark, used for this software
and anything published under its name. They are claimed as unregistered marks
of Halil Öztürkci, the copyright holder named in [NOTICE](NOTICE).

## What you may do without asking

- **Say what your thing is.** "Built on DFIR Swarm", "works with DFIR Swarm",
  "a plugin for DFIR Swarm", "we ran DFIR Swarm on this case". Use the name
  truthfully to refer to this project.
- **Write and talk about it.** Articles, papers, courses, conference talks,
  screenshots, reviews, criticism and comparisons. You do not need our
  permission and you do not need to be nice about it.
- **Redistribute it unchanged** under its own name, with the LICENSE and
  NOTICE files intact.
- **Use the name in a report.** Naming the tool and its version in a forensic
  report, an affidavit or an expert statement is exactly what the name is for.

## What needs permission

- **Naming your own product, service or company after it**, in whole or in
  part, or registering a domain, social account, package or repository whose
  name would be read as this project.
- **Shipping a modified version under this name.** Fork freely, and give the
  fork its own name. A build that is not this build must not answer to the
  same name, because the point of the name is to say which code ran.
- **Anything that implies endorsement, affiliation or certification** that
  does not exist, including "official", "certified" and "approved".
- **Using the wordmark as your own logo**, or as an element of one.

## Forking

Renaming a fork is a small change and this is the whole of it: give the
project a different name, do not use the wordmark, and say plainly what you
started from. "Based on DFIR Swarm" is accurate and welcome; being called
DFIR Swarm is not. Nothing in this policy limits what the licence already
allows you to do with the code itself, and section 7 lets a recipient who
finds this term unacceptable remove it from their own copy.
